Five costumes, one grid

Five Zarr stores on object storage hold the same seven days of Antarctic sea ice (University of Bremen ASI, AMSR2, 3125 m) on the same grid: NSIDC polar stereographic south, 2528 by 2656 cells. Each store says where that grid is in a different way. The letters are the costumes of Regular in what?: the storage form a store presents, not the grid it holds. This page reads every store's metadata from the bucket, applies four common reader rules to each, and then draws the stores it can place.

A transform needs nothing beyond the metadata. A CF grid also needs its 1-D x and y axes, which are coordinate data but only a few kilobytes; the card reads them to confirm they are regular, and, where a store also declares a transform, that the two agree. The fifth costume would need 68 MB of 2-D longitude and latitude, and then an inference step. Instead, the assertion demo shows a separate, published document supplying the grid for f03d. With it applied, f03d draws identically to f03e.

The costume letters are those of storecard.card() (see FINDINGS.md), from chapter 3 of Regular in what?. The rule with fixed names stands for readers that look for a grid mapping array called crs or spatial_ref rather than following CF's grid_mapping attribute, as the Zarr profile draft does (portolan-sdi/portolan-spec PR 234).

This page is a minimal reader, not a general viewer: Zarr v3, bytes and zstd codecs, axis-aligned affine grids, and one known CRS, recognised by its parameters in each of its spellings.
