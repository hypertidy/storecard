One assertion, end to end

Two Zarr stores on object storage hold the same seven days of Antarctic sea ice (University of Bremen ASI, AMSR2, 3125 m). f03e declares its grid: six numbers and a CRS. f03d hides the same grid behind 2-D longitude and latitude arrays, so a reader that wants a declared grid refuses it. A published assertion supplies the six numbers and the CRS without touching f03d. With it applied, f03d draws exactly as f03e does. This page reads both stores directly from the bucket, decodes the chunks itself, and applies the assertion as an overlay.

Check the evidence yourself

The assertion records how it was made: storecard.fit projected a sample of the lon/lat cell centres into a short list of published frames and kept the one where every centre lands within 0.001 cell of the asserted grid. This button repeats the check over every cell, in the browser: it fetches f03d's lon and lat, projects each pair to EPSG:3412 (polar stereographic south, Hughes 1980 ellipsoid, true scale at 70 S) and measures the distance to the asserted cell centre.

The assertion

It is a separate document, keyed to the store's URL, the variable and a digest of the store's metadata. Its asserts block holds only zarr-conventions spatial: and proj: keys, so a reader can lay it over the array's attributes as they stand. The source store is never edited.
