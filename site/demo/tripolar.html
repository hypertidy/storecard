An honest refusal

An ocean model (ACCESS-ESM1.6, MOM5) computes on a tripolar grid: a latitude-longitude grid south of about 65 N, and north of it a cap whose two poles sit on land, in Canada and Siberia, so the ocean has no singular point. The model writes 1-D axes xt_ocean and yt_ocean beside the true 2-D positions geolon_t and geolat_t. The 1-D axes are exact in the south and wrong in the cap. The store here, f08c, carries both, as CF says it should. A grid fit that tries every published frame finds that no six numbers describe this grid, and says so instead of asserting one. This page reads the store from the bucket and shows why.

Where the 1-D axes go wrong

For each grid row: the largest distance, over its 360 cells, between the true position and the 1-D axes' position, in cells. This is the measure behind the card's NOMINAL_AXES_DISAGREE finding, here over every row instead of the card's sample lines.

The refusal

storecard.fit projects the card's coordinate sample, the edges of the grid plus a central cross, into each frame on its list, fits an affine transform from cell index to projected position, and keeps a frame only if every sampled cell lies within 0.001 cell of it. For f03d, the first frame fits to within 5e-10 cells. For f08c, none comes within hundreds of cells. In the south, yt_ocean's steps change from about 1/4 degree near the equator to 1 degree, so no frame is affine there either. Then the cap bends away from every frame on the list.

loading the refusal

A refusal is published like an assertion, keyed to the store's URL and a digest of its metadata. It records which frames were tried, so a later fit, for example in degredation with frames outside this list, starts from it rather than repeating it. It asserts nothing about the store, which is a correct CF description of the grid. It tells a reader that six numbers will not do here. To draw f08c in a map, a reader needs the 2-D positions (a per-cell warp), or a regridded copy such as f08d.
